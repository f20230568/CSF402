How the board is drawn
  1. Grid & octilinear traces. Vertices are pads on grid points; edges are traces of unit steps in 8 directions (multiples of 45°).
  2. Gender rule (from the metro map).
    Female: last segment horizontal / vertical
    Male: last segment diagonal
    Segments may have unequal lengths: straight (a), one 45° bend (a, b), a dogleg (a, c, b) or a chamfered corner (a 90° turn cut by a 45° segment, as on a real board).
  3. Routing. Each tree grows from its centre (or the root node), generation by generation, largest sub-tree first. Every candidate trace is scored:
    cost = length − bus·(steps parallel to a neighbouring trace) + 0.7·bends + compactness·Δ(box perimeter) − line continuation + look-ahead
    The bus bonus makes traces bundle on adjacent grid lines and turn together. Traces never touch pads or other traces and never cross.
  4. Squeeze. The sub-trees touching one side of the board are ripped up and routed again inside a box one step smaller; repeated on all four sides while everything still fits without conflicts.
  5. Forest packing by footprint. Trees are placed largest first where the board stays smallest for the chosen aspect ratio, using their real occupied grid points, so small trees nest into the gaps of big ones.
  6. Verification. The finished board is checked independently: pad collisions and clearance, traces through pads, overlapping or crossing traces, and the gender rule for every arrival.
A pad has at most 8 directions, so a vertex with more than 8 edges cannot be drawn without conflicts (they are reported).
